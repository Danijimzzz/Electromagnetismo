The metal detector

You sweep the sand with a disc on the end of a pole and suddenly it beeps: there is a coin fifteen centimetres under your feet. The detector does not "see" the metal; it is enough to tickle it with a magnetic field and listen to how it answers.

A little history

  1. 1831Michael Faraday discovers electromagnetic induction: a changing magnetic field induces a voltage in any nearby conductor. Without that law there would be no detectors.
  2. 1881Alexander Graham Bell builds an "induction balance" to locate the bullet lodged in the body of President Garfield. It did not work, because the metal bedspring confused the device.
  3. Around 1930Gerhard Fisher patents the first portable metal detector, designed to look for deposits and buried pipes.
  4. 1941The Polish engineer Józef Kosacki develops a portable mine detector that is used in the Battle of El Alamein, in 1942.
  5. Since 1960Transistors make them light and cheap and the hobby of searching for coins and relics is born; the same principle today opens the security gates at airports.

Which tool it relates to

Self-inductance and mutual inductance · Electromagnetism

The coin and the coil are coupled by their mutual inductance $M$: the voltage that appears in the coin is $M$ times how fast the coil current changes, and $M$ falls with depth $z$.

M = μ₀ π N₁ N₂ R² r² / 2(R² + z²)3/2 ≈ 25.1 nH

ε = M · dI/dt = M · 2π f I₀ ≈ 0.79 mV

It opens in the mutual inductance tab with two coaxial loops (10 cm radius and 25 turns the coil, 1.2 cm and one turn the coin) 10 cm apart, with a current of 0.5 A at 10 kHz. You can change the distance, the size of the coin or the turns and see how M, the flux and the induced voltage change. The coin is treated as a single loop without resistance and the data are indicative.
