The bird perched on a cable

A high-voltage cable kills a person in an instant, but birds perch on them and do not even flinch. Why are they not electrocuted?

A little history

  1. 1827Georg Simon Ohm publishes that the current through a conductor is proportional to the voltage across its ends: V = I·R. At first almost nobody paid attention.
  2. 1845Gustav Kirchhoff, still a student, states the laws of nodes and loops, which divide the current among parallel branches such as the cable and the bird.
  3. 1891The first alternating-current high-voltage line opens, from Lauffen to Frankfurt: about 175 km at 15 kV. Raising the voltage makes it possible to carry the same power with less current and fewer losses.
  4. 2008In Spain, Royal Decree 1432/2008 requires high-voltage lines to be protected in areas with threatened birds, because the eagles and storks that touch the cable and the tower at the same time die by electrocution.

Which tool it relates to

Circuit simulator · Circuits

The bird and the piece of cable between its feet are two resistors in parallel through which the line current is divided. It is a problem of Ohm's law and nodes.

ΔV = I R_{cable} , I_{bird} = ΔV/R_{bird}

I_{bird}/I_{cable} = R_{cable}/R_{bird}

It opens with a 500 A current source (the line), the cable between the feet (R1 = 5 µΩ), the bird in parallel (R2 = 10 kΩ) and an 800 Ω load that leaves the cable at 400 kV. We get a voltage of 2.5 mV between the feet and 0.25 µA through the bird.
