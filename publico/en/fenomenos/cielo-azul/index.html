The blue sky

Sunlight is white and space is black, but the daytime sky is blue and the sunset one, red. There is no paint in the air: it is light and molecules, and it is calculated with a single power.

A little history

  1. 1672Isaac Newton publishes his experiments with the prism (carried out around 1666): white light contains all the colours; until then it was thought that the prism "painted" them.
  2. 1869John Tyndall passes light through gases with very small particles and sees that the scattered light is blue, like the sky. He believed it was done by dust or vapour particles.
  3. 1871Lord Rayleigh deduces that light scattered by particles much smaller than the wavelength is proportional to 1/λ⁴.
  4. 1899Rayleigh concludes that dust is not needed: the air molecules themselves are enough to explain the blue of the sky.
  5. 1910Albert Einstein calculates the scattering of light by air due to density fluctuations and links it to Avogadro's number.

Which tool it relates to

Electromagnetic waves · Electromagnetism

Light is a plane electromagnetic wave and its colour is its wavelength. With it come the frequency that makes the dipole oscillate and the wavenumber; Rayleigh scattering depends on the fourth power of either.

I ∝ 1/λ^4 ∝ ω^4

I_blue / I_red = (700/450)^4 ≈ 5.9

It opens in plane-wave-in-vacuum mode, with the 450 nm blue light: the frequency comes out 6.66·10¹⁴ Hz and the wavenumber, 1.40·10⁷ rad/m. Change the wavelength to 700 nm and check it with red.
