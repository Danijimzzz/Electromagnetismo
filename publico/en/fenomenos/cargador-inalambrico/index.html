The wireless charger

You put the phone on a base and it starts charging, with no cable or contact. There is no magic: it is Faraday's law as ever, applied to two coils a few millimetres apart.

A little history

  1. 1831Michael Faraday discovers electromagnetic induction: a changing magnetic field creates a voltage in a nearby circuit, without touching it. It is the principle behind every wireless charger.
  2. 1891Nikola Tesla presents his resonant coil and dreams of transmitting power without wires over great distances. On a large scale it never worked, but resonance is key in chargers today.
  3. Late 20th centuryThe first electric toothbrushes appear that charge by induction on their base, sealed so they can be used in the bathroom.
  4. 2008 and 2010The wireless charging consortium is born and publishes the Qi standard (which means "energy" in Chinese); in 2010 the first compatible phones arrive.
M

Which tool it relates to

Self-inductance and mutual inductance · Electromagnetism

Energy passes from one coil to the other through their mutual inductance $M$: the voltage that appears in the phone's coil is $M$ times how fast the current in the base changes.

M = μ₀ π N₁ N₂ R² r² / 2(R² + z²)3/2 ≈ 1,52 µH

ε = M · dI/dt = M · 2π f I₀ ≈ 2.1 V

It opens in the mutual inductance tab with two coaxial loops (3 cm and 1 cm radius, 20 turns each) 2 cm apart, with a current of 2 A at 110 kHz. You can change the distance, the number of turns or the size of the coils and see how M, the flux and the induced voltage change. The coil data are indicative.
