Lightning

Lightning is the discharge of a giant capacitor: the base of a cloud and the ground, about 2 km apart, charged to a hundred million volts. How much charge, how much current and how much energy is there in a single lightning bolt?

A little history

  1. 1752At Marly-la-Ville, near Paris, Thomas-François d'Alibard collects sparks from a cloud with an iron rod, as Benjamin Franklin had proposed. Soon after, Franklin performs his kite experiment and proposes the lightning rod: lightning is electricity.
  2. 1920C. T. R. Wilson measures the electric field under thunderstorms and deduces that a thundercloud is a dipole, with the top positive and the bottom negative.
  3. 1934The physicists Basil Schonland and Hugh Collens, in South Africa, photograph with a rotating-lens camera the stepped leader that descends in jumps and the return stroke that rises.
  4. TodayCameras that record hundreds of thousands of frames per second follow each jump of the leader, and how a lightning strike starts inside the cloud is still being studied.

Which tool it relates to

Dielectrics and capacitors · Electromagnetism

The base of the cloud and the ground are the two plates of a parallel-plate capacitor with air. Its capacitance depends only on the geometry; the charge and the energy, on the voltage it is charged to.

C = {ε_0 S}/{d} , Q = C V

U = 1/2 Q V = 1/2 C V^2

It opens with a parallel-plate capacitor of 10,000,000 m² (10 km²) and 2000 m separation, filled with air and connected to 100 MV. We get a capacitance of 44.27 nF, a charge of 4.427 C and an energy of 221.4 MJ.
