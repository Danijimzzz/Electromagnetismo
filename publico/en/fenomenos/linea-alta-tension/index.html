The high-voltage line and the ground

If you hold a fluorescent tube under a high-voltage line, it lights up by itself, with no cables or socket. The cable and the ground form an enormous capacitor.

A little history

  1. 1745Ewald von Kleist and Pieter van Musschenbroek independently invent the Leyden jar, the first capacitor: two conductors separated by an insulator that store charge.
  2. 1848William Thomson, the future Lord Kelvin, invents the method of images: the field of a charge next to a conducting plane is that of the charge and an "image" of opposite sign on the other side.
  3. 1891The first three-phase high-voltage line carries electricity from Lauffen to Frankfurt, 175 km away, at more than 15,000 V. Since then, lines carry energy as alternating current at high voltage.
  4. 2004The artist Richard Box plants 1,301 fluorescent tubes in a field under a high-voltage line, near Bristol. At dusk they light up on their own with the electric field of the line.
E

Which tool it relates to

Electrostatics · Electromagnetism

By the method of images, the cable above the conducting ground is the same as two infinite parallel wires with opposite charges, the cable and its image, separated by 2h. The potential and the field come from adding those of the two wires.

C′ = {2πε_0}/{ln(2h/a)} , λ = C′ V

E_"ground" = {λ}/{πε_0 h}

It opens with the two wires in SI, 10 m above and below the ground and with ±1.028 µC/m. We get a potential difference of 133 kV between the cable and the ground, a field of 3.7 kV/m at the ground and 3.8 kV/m at 1.5 m height, and 4.5 kV between the ends of the fluorescent tube.
