The pole transformer

At the top of a pole there is a grey box that hums. 20,000 V goes in and 400 V comes out, and it has no moving parts. How does it change the voltage with just two coils and a piece of iron, and why does it hum?

A little history

  1. 1831Michael Faraday discovers induction with two coils wound on an iron ring: it is, without knowing it, the first transformer.
  2. 1836Nicholas Callan builds an induction coil in Ireland, which produces very high voltages from a battery.
  3. 1885Károly Zipernowsky, Miksa Déri and Ottó Bláthy present in Budapest the first closed-core transformer, which makes it possible to supply many lamps from a single line.
  4. 1886George Westinghouse and William Stanley test in Great Barrington (Massachusetts) the first alternating-current network with transformers.
  5. Late 19th centuryIn the "war of the currents", alternating current beats direct current precisely because its voltage can be raised and lowered with a transformer.

Which tool it relates to

Self-inductance and mutual inductance · Electromagnetism

An ideal transformer is two coils with perfect coupling (k = 1): the emf of the secondary is the mutual inductance M times how fast the primary current changes, and its ratio to that of the primary is the turns ratio.

ε₂ = −M dI₁/dt , M = μ₀ N₁ N₂ h ln(b/a) / 2π , ε₂/ε₁ = N₂/N₁ = 1/50

It opens in the mutual inductance tab, in the "Coil on a toroid (k = 1)" geometry: N₁ = 2500, N₂ = 50, a core of 8 to 12 cm radius and 4 cm height, without iron (with iron L and M are multiplied by μr, but the ratio is the same). With the rate of change of the primary corresponding to 20 kV peak, we get ε₂ ≈ 566 V peak, that is, 400 V rms.
