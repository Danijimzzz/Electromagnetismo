The airbag and the seat belt

In a crash at 50 km/h the car stops in an instant. With a seat belt you walk away; without it, the same crash can kill you. The difference lies in a few centimetres.

A little history

  1. 1687Isaac Newton publishes his Principia with the law of inertia: a body keeps moving in a straight line as long as no force stops it. This is what happens to you inside a car that crashes.
  2. 1953The American engineer John Hetrick patents an "air bag" that inflates in a crash, after a scare in the car with his family.
  3. 1959Nils Bohlin, an engineer at Volvo, designs the three-point seat belt that all cars carry today. Volvo leaves the patent free for any manufacturer to use.
  4. 1981Mercedes-Benz fits the driver's airbag in a production car, the S-Class, always together with the seat belt: the airbag only works well if the belt holds you.

Which tool it relates to

Work and energy · General physics

The force that slows you down does negative work that removes all your kinetic energy. The longer the braking distance, the smaller the average force.

W = −F d = 0 − 1/2 m v^2

F = {m v^2}/{2 d}

It opens in the variable force tab, with a force of −14,700 N over 0.5 m on a 75 kg person travelling at 14 m/s. The work comes out −7350 J and the final speed, zero: the belt stops you exactly in the distance the car crumples.
