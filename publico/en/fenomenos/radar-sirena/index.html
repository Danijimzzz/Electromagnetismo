The traffic radar and the siren

An ambulance siren sounds higher when it approaches and lower when it leaves, even though the siren always emits the same. And a radar knows how fast you are going just by listening to the echo of its waves. What do they have in common?

A little history

  1. 1842Christian Doppler, a professor in Prague, proposes that the colour of double stars changes depending on whether they approach or recede from us. For stars he was wrong about the size of the effect, but the idea was good.
  2. 1845Christophorus Buys Ballot tests it with sound in Utrecht: he puts musicians on a train passing at full speed playing a fixed note, and other musicians on the platform, with a good ear, notice how the pitch changes.
  3. 1868William Huggins measures the shift of the lines in the spectrum of Sirius and calculates how fast it is moving: the Doppler effect becomes a tool of astronomy.
  4. 1947The first speed radars for the police appear in the United States. Today traffic ones work at around 24 GHz or 34 GHz, or with laser.

Which tool it relates to

Doppler effect · General physics

The siren is a source moving towards a stationary observer, and the radar is an echo off a moving obstacle: two Doppler effects in a row, first the car as observer and then as source.

f′ = f v/(v − v_F) , f″ = f (v + u)/(v − u)

Δf = f″ − f ≈ 2 u f/c

It opens in the echo tab with the stationary 24 GHz radar, waves at 3·108 m/s and the car approaching at 33 m/s. We get an echo 5280 Hz higher, the difference the radar measures.
