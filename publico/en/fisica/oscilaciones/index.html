Oscillations: SHM, damped and forced

Calculate the period, the energy and the position of a spring or a pendulum, how an oscillator with friction dies out and how much an oscillator driven by a periodic force is amplified near its resonance.

    Simple harmonic motion

    A body of mass $m$ attached to a spring of constant $k$ experiences a force $F = −k x$, and its equation $m x″ = −k x$ has the solution $x = A cos(ω t + φ)$ with $ω = √(k/m)$. The period $T = 2π/ω$ does not depend on the amplitude. The velocity is $v = −A ω sin(ω t + φ)$, the acceleration $a = −ω^2 x$, and the total energy $E = 1/2 k A^2$ passes from kinetic to potential without being lost.

    The simple pendulum

    For small angles, $sin θ ≈ θ$ and the pendulum obeys $ω = √(g/L)$, independent of the mass. With large amplitudes the real period is longer; the tool calculates it with the arithmetic–geometric mean and compares it with the approximate one.

    Damped oscillator

    With a friction $−b v$ the equation is $m x″ + b x′ + k x = 0$. With $γ = b/2m$ and $ω_0 = √(k/m)$ there are three cases: if $γ < ω_0$ it oscillates with $ω_d = √(ω_0^2 − γ^2)$ inside an envelope $e^{−γ t}$; if $γ = ω_0$ it is critical and returns to equilibrium as fast as possible without oscillating (this is how door dampers are designed); if $γ > ω_0$ it is overdamped and returns slowly. The quality factor $Q = ω_0/(2γ)$ measures how lightly damped it is.

    Forced oscillator and resonance

    With a force $F_0 cos(ω t)$, after the transient the body oscillates at the frequency of the force with amplitude $A = {F_0/m}/√((ω_0^2 − ω^2)^2 + 4γ^2ω^2)$, lagging by an angle $δ$ with respect to it. The amplitude is greatest near $ω_r = √(ω_0^2 − 2γ^2)$: this is resonance, the narrower and taller the lower the friction. It is what makes a glass ring to the voice, or what is avoided in bridges and in buildings in earthquakes.

    Frequently asked questions

    How are the frequency and period of an oscillator calculated?

    Spring: ω = √(k/m) and T = 2π√(m/k); simple pendulum: T = 2π√(L/g). With k = 100 N/m and m = 1 kg, ω = 10 rad/s and T ≈ 0.63 s; a 1 m pendulum has T ≈ 2.0 s.

    What is the difference between an underdamped, critically damped and overdamped oscillator?

    With friction b and mass m, if b < 2√(km) the system oscillates with frequency ω′ = √(ω₀² − (b/2m)²) and decreasing amplitude; if b = 2√(km) it returns to equilibrium without oscillating and as fast as possible; if b > 2√(km) it returns slowly. With an external force, there is resonance when ω approaches the natural frequency.
