    The Boltzmann factor and the partition function

    A system in equilibrium with a bath at temperature $T$ occupies a state of energy $ε_i$ with probability proportional to $e^{−ε_i/k_B T}$. If the level has $g_i$ states, its weight is multiplied by $g_i$. Everything follows from the partition function:

    $Z = Σ g_i·e^{−ε_i/k_B T}$, $p_i = {g_i·e^{−ε_i/k_B T}}/Z$

    $U = k_B T^2 {d ln Z}/{dT}$, $F = −k_B T ln Z$, $S = k_B (ln Z + U/{k_B T})$

    The heat capacity $C_V = dU/dT$ is the variance of the energy divided by $k_B T^2$. The energy is measured from the ground level; changing the origin does not change the populations.

    Two levels and oscillators

    With two levels separated by $ΔE$, the heat capacity has a maximum (the Schottky anomaly) when $k_B T$ is around $ΔE$. The quantum harmonic oscillator has levels $E_n = (n + 1/2)hν$ and $Z = 1/{1 − e^{−hν/k_B T}}$. Einstein used it for a solid: at high temperature $C_V → 3R$ (Dulong-Petit) and at low temperature the modes "freeze out" and $C_V → 0$. For a rigid rotor $E_J = B J(J+1)$ with $g = 2J+1$ and, at high temperature, $Z ≈ k_B T/B$.

    Entropy and microstates

    $S = k_B ln Ω$

    An Einstein solid with $N$ oscillators and $q$ quanta has $Ω = binom(q + N − 1, q)$ microstates. The temperature comes from $1/T = ∂S/∂U$. If two solids share quanta, the distribution with the most microstates overwhelmingly dominates and that is where the temperatures become equal: it is the second law seen as pure counting. The Maxwell distribution of the speeds of a gas is in Ideal gases and cycles with entropy are in Thermodynamic cycles.

    What this tool does not cover

    This tool uses Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics: each level has a population proportional to $g·e^{−ε/k_B T}$, valid for distinguishable particles (such as the oscillators of an Einstein solid) or for a dilute gas in which almost no state is occupied by more than one particle. There are no Fermi-Dirac statistics (fermions: only one electron per state, like the electrons in a metal) or Bose-Einstein statistics (bosons: photons, helium-4, condensates). This is a deliberate decision: those distributions need the chemical potential $μ$ and the density of states of the system, which depend on the total number of particles and on the geometry, and cannot be reduced to a list of levels with degeneracy. When the mean occupation number is much less than 1 (high temperature or low density) all three coincide with Maxwell-Boltzmann, which is the only case calculated here.

    Limits of this tool

    • It starts from discrete energy levels and the Boltzmann distribution (distinguishable particles or the levels of a system). It does not include Fermi-Dirac or Bose-Einstein statistics: it is not valid for degenerate quantum gases.
    • The Einstein solid gives a single frequency for all the oscillators: it does not reproduce the Debye model at low temperature.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the partition function and what can you get from it?

    Z = Σ gᵢ·e^(−εᵢ/k_BT) adds up the Boltzmann weight of all the levels. From it, pᵢ = gᵢ·e^(−εᵢ/k_BT)/Z, U = k_BT²·d(ln Z)/dT, F = −k_BT·ln Z and S = k_B(ln Z + U/k_BT). With two non-degenerate levels and ΔE = k_BT, Z = 1 + e⁻¹ = 1.368 and the excited level has p = 0.269.

    How do you calculate entropy by counting microstates?

    S = k_B·ln Ω. In an Einstein solid with N oscillators and q quanta, Ω = (q + N − 1)!/(q!·(N − 1)!). With N = 3 and q = 4, Ω = 15 and S = k_B·ln 15 = 2.71 k_B; the temperature comes from 1/T = ∂S/∂U.
