    The International System

    The SI has seven base units: metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela. All other quantities are expressed with them: the newton is $"kg·m/s²"$, the joule is $"N·m"$ and the tesla is $"kg/(A·s²)"$. Converting between units of the same quantity means multiplying by a factor equal to 1.

    Conversion factors

    If $1 "mi" = 1609.344 "m"$, then $(1609.344 "m")/(1 "mi") = 1$ and you can multiply by that quotient (or its inverse) without changing the quantity, only the unit. This tool always goes through the SI base unit: first to metres, kilograms, joules… and from there to the requested unit, and at the end it gives you the direct factor.

    Temperatures

    The Celsius and Fahrenheit scales have their zero shifted, so multiplying is not enough: $T_K = T_C + 273.15$ and $T_F = T_C · 9/5 + 32$. Temperature differences are converted with a factor ($1 "K" = 1 "°C"$ and $1 "K" = 1.8 "°F"$). Nothing exists below $0 "K"$.

    SI prefixes

    Each prefix is a power of 10: $"p" = 10^{−12}$, $"n" = 10^{−9}$, $"µ" = 10^{−6}$, $"m" = 10^{−3}$, $"k" = 10^3$, $"M" = 10^6$, $"G" = 10^9$, $"T" = 10^{12}$. A typical mistake is confusing $"mm"$ with $"Mm"$ or $"mV"$ with $"MV"$.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do you convert units?

    You multiply by the conversion factor so that the starting unit cancels. For example, 90 km/h = 90 / 3.6 = 25 m/s; and 1 atm = 101,325 Pa, 1 cal = 4.184 J, 1 eV = 1.602·10⁻¹⁹ J.

    What values do the most commonly used constants have?

    c = 299,792,458 m/s, h = 6.626·10⁻³⁴ J·s, e = 1.602·10⁻¹⁹ C, k_B = 1.381·10⁻²³ J/K and N_A = 6.022·10²³ mol⁻¹, according to CODATA.
