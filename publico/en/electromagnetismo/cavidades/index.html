    What a resonant cavity is

    A closed metal box (or a piece of waveguide closed at both ends) in which an electromagnetic wave is trapped and forms a standing wave. Only certain modes exist, each with its own resonant frequency $f_0$ and field shape. It is the microwave equivalent of the $L C$ circuit: it stores electric and magnetic energy that are exchanged at $f_0$. It is used in filters, oscillators, frequency meters (wavemeters), particle accelerators and microwave ovens.

    Resonant frequency

    Rectangular $a × b × d$, with modes $"TE"_{mnp}$ ($p ≥ 1$) and $"TM"_{mnp}$ ($m, n ≥ 1$):

    $f_0 = v/2 √({(m/a)^2 + (n/b)^2 + (p/d)^2})$,   $v = c/√(ε_r)$

    Cylindrical of radius $R$ and length $L$, with $x_{nm}$ the $m$-th zero of $J_n$ (TM modes, $p ≥ 0$) or $x′_{nm}$ that of $J′_n$ (TE modes, $p ≥ 1$):

    $f_0 = v/{2π} √({(x_{nm}/R)^2 + (p π/L)^2})$

    Spherical of radius $R$: for TE modes, $x_{nl}$ is the $l$-th zero of $j_n$; for TM modes, that of $(x j_n)′$:

    $f_0 = {v x_{nl}}/{2π R}$

    The dominant rectangular mode is $"TE"_{101}$; in the cylindrical cavity, $"TM"_{010}$ (if $L < 2.03 R$) or $"TE"_{111}$; in the spherical one, $"TM"_{11}$ ($x = 2.744$). The $"TE"_{0mp}$ and $"TM"_{1mp}$ modes of the cylindrical cavity are degenerate.

    Quality factor

    $Q = ω_0 ·$ (stored energy) $/$ (power lost). Walls of conductivity $σ$ dissipate by Joule heating in a layer of thickness $δ = 1/√(π f_0 μ_0 σ)$, with surface resistance $R_s = 1/(σ δ)$:

    $Q_c = {ω_0 μ_0 ∫|H|^2 dV}/{R_s ∮|H_t|^2 dS} = {2Γ}/δ$

    A lossy filling adds $Q_d = 1/{"tan" δ}$ and the two combine in parallel:

    $1/Q = 1/Q_c + 1/Q_d$,   $Δf = f_0/Q$

    A high $Q$ means a very narrow resonance. In an air-filled copper cavity the order of magnitude is $Q ≈ V/(S δ)$, from thousands to tens of thousands; in the spherical one, the TE modes have exactly $Q_c = R/δ$.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is the resonant frequency of a rectangular cavity calculated?

    f₀ = (v/2)·√((m/a)² + (n/b)² + (p/d)²), with v = c/√εr. For example, an air-filled cube 40 mm on a side in the TE101 mode resonates at (c/2·0.04)·√2 = 5.300 GHz; the TE011 and the TM110 have the same value (degenerate modes).

    What is the quality factor Q of a cavity and how is it calculated?

    Q = ω₀·(stored energy)/(power lost) and measures how narrow the resonance is: Δf = f₀/Q. The walls give Q_c = 2Γ/δ, with δ = 1/√(π f₀ μ₀ σ) and Γ = ∫|H|²dV / ∮|H_t|²dS; the dielectric gives Q_d = 1/tan δ, and they combine as 1/Q = 1/Q_c + 1/Q_d. A copper cylindrical cavity with R = 25 mm and L = 40 mm in the TM010 mode resonates at 4.590 GHz with Q_c ≈ 15 800.
