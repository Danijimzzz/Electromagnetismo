    The Laplace equation

    In a region without charges the electric potential satisfies $∇² V = 0$. Its solutions are harmonic functions: they have no maxima or minima in the interior, so the highest and lowest potentials are always on the boundary. And the solution is completely fixed once the potential on the whole boundary is known (uniqueness theorem).

    Mean value property

    The potential at a point is the mean of its values over a circle around it. On a square grid this means that each node equals the mean of its four neighbours:

    $V_{i,j} = {V_{i+1,j} + V_{i−1,j} + V_{i,j+1} + V_{i,j−1}}/4$

    Relaxation

    You start with any distribution and replace each node by the mean of its neighbours until it stops changing. Jacobi computes all nodes with the values of the previous sweep; Gauss-Seidel already uses the new values and takes half as long; SOR overshoots the mean value a little (over-relaxation, with $1 < ω < 2$) and for a grid of $N$ cells needs of the order of $N$ sweeps instead of $N^2$. Compare the three with the method selector.

    Electric field

    Once the potential is known, the field is its gradient with the sign changed, $E = −∇V$: it is perpendicular to the equipotentials and points where the potential decreases. On a conductor (the electrode) the field leaves perpendicular to its surface.

    Poisson equation

    With charge in the region, $∇² V = −ρ/ε_0$. Relaxation only changes in the source term: $V_{i,j} = {V_{i+1,j} + V_{i−1,j} + V_{i,j+1} + V_{i,j−1}}/4 + {ρ h^2}/{4 ε_0}$. If the charge is uniform between two grounded plates, $V(x) = {ρ x (a − x)}/{2 ε_0}$.

    Insulating edges, charge and capacitance

    At an insulating edge the field does not cross it ($∂V/∂n = 0$) and it is solved with a mirror node. The charge of each conductor comes from Gauss's flux, $Q' = ε_0 ∮ E_n dl$, and the capacitance per metre of two conductors is $C' = Q'/ΔV$, which also equals $2W'/ΔV^2$ with the energy $W' = ½ ε_0 ∫ E^2 dA$.

    Exact series solution

    For a box of sides $a$ and $b$ with a single edge (the top one) at $V_0$ and the others at 0, the exact solution is a Fourier series, with $n$ odd:

    $V(x, y) = ∑ {4V_0}/{nπ} · sen({nπx}/a) · {senh({nπy}/a)}/{senh({nπb}/a)}$

    The other edges contribute the same rotated, and by superposition it holds for any combination. The tool uses it to measure the grid error when there is no electrode.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is the Laplace equation solved by relaxation?

    The box is divided into a grid and each interior point is replaced by the mean of its four neighbours, V(i,j) = ¼[V(i+1,j) + V(i−1,j) + V(i,j+1) + V(i,j−1)], repeating until the change is smaller than the tolerance. Jacobi uses the values of the previous iteration, Gauss-Seidel the most recent ones, and SOR speeds them up with a factor between 1 and 2.

    Can the result be checked by hand?

    Yes, in symmetric cases. If one edge of a square box is at 100 V and the other three at 0 V, the potential at the centre is the mean of the four edges, 25 V, and the numerical solution should approach that value.
