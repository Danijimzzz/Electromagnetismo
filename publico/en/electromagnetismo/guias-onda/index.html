    What a waveguide is

    A hollow metal tube (or one filled with dielectric) of rectangular cross-section $a × b$, with $a ≥ b$, that carries microwave electromagnetic waves with little loss. The walls are treated as a perfect conductor: the tangential E field vanishes on them, and so only certain modes fit, with $m$ and $n$ half-cycles along $a$ and $b$.

    TE and TM modes

    In TE modes the electric field is transverse to the propagation ($E_z = 0$) and in TM modes the magnetic field is ($H_z = 0$). For TE, $m, n ≥ 0$ (not both zero at once) and for TM $m, n ≥ 1$. The mode with the lowest cutoff frequency, the dominant one, is $"TE"_{10}$.

    Cutoff frequency

    $f_c = v/2 √({(m/a)^2 + (n/b)^2})$,   $λ_c = v/f_c$,   $v = c/√(ε_r)$

    Below $f_c$ the mode does not propagate: its field decays with $α = k_c √(1 − (f/f_c)^2)$ and carries no power. Above it, it propagates with

    $β = k √(1 − (f_c/f)^2)$,   $λ_g = {2π}/β > λ$,   $v_f = v/√(1 − (f_c/f)^2) > v$,   $v_g = v √(1 − (f_c/f)^2) < v$

    The phase velocity exceeds that of the medium, but the energy travels at the group velocity, and $v_f v_g = v^2$. The wave impedances are $Z_{"TE"} = η/√(1 − (f_c/f)^2)$ and $Z_{"TM"} = η √(1 − (f_c/f)^2)$, with $η = η_0/√(ε_r)$.

    Single-mode band

    Between the cutoff frequency of the dominant mode and that of the next one, only one mode propagates, which is how it is used in practice. In a WR-90 guide (22.86 × 10.16 mm) the $"TE"_{10}$ cuts off at 6.557 GHz and the next mode at 13.114 GHz ($"TE"_{20}$): the X band (8 to 12 GHz) falls inside the single-mode band.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is the cutoff frequency of a rectangular waveguide calculated?

    For the TE_mn mode, f_c = (c/2)·√((m/a)² + (n/b)²). For the fundamental TE₁₀ mode of a WR-90 guide with a = 22.86 mm, f_c = c/(2a) ≈ 6.56 GHz.

    How is the wavelength in the guide calculated?

    It only propagates if f > f_c, and then λ_g = λ/√(1 − (f_c/f)²). At 10 GHz, λ = 3 cm and f_c = 6.56 GHz: λ_g = 3 / √(1 − 0.43) ≈ 3.97 cm.
