    What happens at the boundary

    A wave reaching the surface separating two media splits into a reflected wave and a transmitted one. The angles obey the law of reflection, $θ_r = θ_i$, and Snell's law, $n_1 sin θ_i = n_2 sin θ_t$, with $n = √(ε_r μ_r)$. What shares the amplitude between the two waves is decided by the boundary conditions: the tangential components of E and H are continuous.

    ⊥ (TE) and ∥ (TM) polarization

    The plane of incidence is the one containing the ray and the normal. If E is perpendicular to it the wave is TE (⊥); if E lies in it, it is TM (∥). Any other polarization splits into these two, which reflect differently. With the impedance of each medium $η = √(μ/ε)$:

    $r_⊥ = {η_2 cos θ_i − η_1 cos θ_t}/{η_2 cos θ_i + η_1 cos θ_t}$,   $t_⊥ = {2 η_2 cos θ_i}/{η_2 cos θ_i + η_1 cos θ_t}$

    $r_∥ = {η_2 cos θ_t − η_1 cos θ_i}/{η_2 cos θ_t + η_1 cos θ_i}$,   $t_∥ = {2 η_2 cos θ_i}/{η_2 cos θ_t + η_1 cos θ_i}$

    The signs follow the convention of the figure of the E fields: at normal incidence both polarizations give the same $r = (n_1 − n_2)/(n_1 + n_2)$ in non-magnetic media. Other books measure the ∥ field the other way round and their $r_∥$ changes sign.

    Reflectance and transmittance

    The energy that is reflected is $R = r^2$ and the energy transmitted, $T = {η_1 cos θ_t}/{η_2 cos θ_i} t^2$ (the beam changes width on refraction). Always $R + T = 1$. At normal incidence, $R = ({n_1 − n_2}/{n_1 + n_2})^2$: 4 % for air and glass.

    Brewster angle

    There is an angle at which $r_∥ = 0$: all the ∥ light is transmitted and the reflected light comes out ⊥ polarized. In non-magnetic media it is $tan θ_B = n_2/n_1$, and then the reflected and transmitted rays are perpendicular. That is why polarized glasses remove glare from water or the road.

    Total internal reflection

    When passing to a lower-index medium ($n_1 > n_2$), beyond the critical angle $sin θ_c = n_2/n_1$ there is no transmitted ray: $R = 1$. In the second medium an evanescent wave remains that decays over a distance of the order of the wavelength, and the reflected wave undergoes a different phase shift in each polarization. It is the basis of optical fibre and total-reflection prisms.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is the reflectance at normal incidence calculated?

    R = ((n₂ − n₁)/(n₂ + n₁))². From air (n = 1) to glass (n = 1.5): R = (0.5/2.5)² = 0.04, that is, 4 % of the light is reflected.

    What are the Brewster angle and total internal reflection?

    The Brewster angle satisfies tan θ_B = n₂/n₁ and at it light polarized in the plane of incidence is not reflected: from air to glass it is arctan 1.5 ≈ 56.3°. Total internal reflection occurs when passing to a lower-index medium beyond the critical angle sin θ_c = n₂/n₁; from glass to air, θ_c ≈ 41.8°.
