What this electrostatics simulator is

It is a free electrostatics simulator for solving the course problems step by step. You place the sources (point charges, wires, rods, arcs, planes and sheets, spheres, shells, cylinders, rings, discs, conductors and dipoles), choose a point, and the simulator calculates the electric field, the potential, the force on a test charge and the energy, with the full working in symbolic form or with numbers and SI units.

What you can calculate

  • The electric field E at any point, adding the contribution of each source (superposition principle).
  • The potential V and the potential difference between two points.
  • The force on a charge, with Coulomb's law, and the potential energy of a system of charges.
  • The Gauss flux, the E(z) and V(z) profiles and the torque on a dipole.
  • The unknowns of a statement with conditions: the charge that makes the field zero at a point, the one that leaves a charge in equilibrium or the one that gives a required potential.

E = k q / r²,   V = k q / r,   F = q E,   k = 1 / (4π ε₀)

How to use it

Start with an example exercise or create a new one, drag the charges across the canvas and look at the field lines, equipotential surfaces, vectors or the colour map of the potential in the plane or in 3D. Save your problems in «My exercises», export the solution and test yourself with practice mode.

Frequently asked questions

How is the electric field of several point charges calculated?

With the superposition principle: the field at a point is the vector sum of the field of each charge, E = Σ k·qᵢ/rᵢ² along the line joining the charge to the point, with k = 8.99·10⁹ N·m²/C². For example, a 2 nC charge at 3 cm creates E = 8.99·10⁹ · 2·10⁻⁹ / 0.03² ≈ 2.0·10⁴ N/C.

How is Gauss's law used to find the field?

You choose a Gaussian surface with the symmetry of the problem (sphere, cylinder or box) and apply ∮E·dS = Q_int/ε₀. Outside a sphere charged with Q, E = Q/(4πε₀r²); for an infinite wire with density λ, E = λ/(2πε₀r); and for an infinite plane with density σ, E = σ/(2ε₀).

How are the potential and the energy of a system of charges calculated?

The potential is a scalar and is added with its sign, V = Σ k·qᵢ/rᵢ. The energy of the system is the sum over all pairs, U = Σ k·qᵢqⱼ/rᵢⱼ. For example, two 1 nC charges 10 cm apart have U = 8.99·10⁹ · 10⁻¹⁸ / 0.1 ≈ 9.0·10⁻⁸ J.

User guide of the simulator

version

1 · Overview

The simulator solves electrostatics problems: you set up the sources (charges, wires, planes, spheres…), add the questions and get the exact result with its working and the drawing of the field.

  1. 1My exercises: the bar common to all tools, with your saved exercises and the example ones.
  2. 2Top bar: title, symbolic or numeric mode, practice mode, undo and export.
  3. 3Statement of the problem, which you can write or paste.
  4. 4Set-up: sources, unknowns and symbols.
  5. 5Canvas and questions: the drawing of the field and, below it, each question with its result.

Everything is recalculated instantly: change a value, a position or drag a charge on the canvas and the result updates by itself.

Capacitors with dielectrics (capacitance, E, D and P fields, polarization charges and combinations) have their own tool: Capacitors.

2 · Choosing a problem

  • The Examples tab (Example exercises) brings exercises already set up and solved. Click one to open it.
  • Saved are yours: press Save current to save the open exercise, with the title and folder you want (type a new name and the folder is created).
  • The ⋮ button of each exercise opens its options: open, select (to print, copy the link or export several at once), change title and folder, pin to the top, copy the link, export or delete. The ⋮ at the top has the backup, import and export all.
  • The bar is the same in all Ciencify tools; the My exercises button at the top left opens and closes it, and Ctrl+K opens the search of the whole site.

You can play with the examples, but changes are not saved automatically in them: press Save current to keep them as your own.

3 · Seeing the solution

Each question shows its exact result in the form of the textbook: a coefficient times the symbols, divided by π ε₀ aⁿ.

  • Press See step-by-step working to follow each step: the contribution of each source, the sum and the simplification.
  • If the problem has unknowns (for example, an unknown charge), the solved value appears in the Unknown panel, with its own working.
  • Dipole questions also say whether the equilibrium is stable or unstable, and the work needed to rotate it.
  • If a datum is missing or something does not add up, a red warning appears under the statement saying what to check.

4 · The field drawing

  • View: choose the XY, XZ or YZ plane and at what height it is cut (z =), or the 3D view, which is rotated by dragging.
  • Turn on or off the field lines, the equipotentials, the vectors and the colour map of V or of |E|.
  • Test charges: press +q or −q and click on the canvas; the charge moves following the force of the field. Remove deletes them.
  • Drag the charges and points to move them (with Shift they do not snap to the grid). Zoom with the −/+ buttons or with Ctrl+wheel, and Fit view centres everything again.

The E(z), V(z) question draws how the field and the potential change along a line. Move the mouse over the graph to read the values.

5 · Creating and editing a problem

  1. Open a similar example or start from scratch, and press Save current in My exercises when you have something to keep.
  2. Write the title at the top and paste the statement if you want to have it at hand.
  3. Add the sources with the green buttons (+ Charge, + Wire, + Plane, + Ring, + Disc, + Conductor…). The button stays marked: click on the canvas to place the source where you want, or press the button again to put it in its default place.
  4. Fill in each card: the value is a coefficient times a symbol (for example -1 × q0) and positions are in units of a. 5/2, 2*sqrt(3), √7 and pi are accepted.
  5. Add the questions with the buttons of its panel: E field, potential, VB − VA, force, work, dipole, energy, Gauss flux or E(z), V(z) profile. They are also placed with a click on the canvas.

Unknowns

If the statement gives a condition (equilibrium, zero field, a known potential…), mark in Unknown which sources have an unknown value and add the conditions. As many equations as unknowns are needed; E = 0 counts as three.

Symbols and units

  • In Symbols say what each symbol is (charge, linear, surface or volume density, or dipole moment) so that the powers of a come out right.
  • With the Symbolic / Numeric SI selector you choose between results with symbols or with numbers: in SI mode write the values in SI units and lengths in metres.
  • ↶ and ↷ undo and redo any change (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z).

6 · Practice mode

Tick Practice mode in the top bar to train: the solutions are hidden and each question asks for your answer.

  1. Write the result with q, lambda, sigma, rho, a, eps0, pi and sqrt(…), for example q/(4*pi*eps0*a^2). The order of the factors does not matter. In SI mode write the number, such as 8.99e9.
  2. Press Check (or Enter): you will see ✓ if it is correct or ✗ if not, with a comment.
  3. Show solution shows the result and the working of that question whenever you want.

7 · Saving, sharing and exporting

  • My exercises are saved in this browser when you press Save current (or Save changes, the round button next to it, if it is already saved). You sort them with folders.
  • To move them to another computer use Export in the ⋮ of the exercise (or Export all at the top) and open them there with Import file… or by dragging the file onto the bar. Copy link also works: it opens the simulator with that exercise. Older .emsim.json files can still be opened.
  • Export solution generates the full solution, with statement, results and working: as a page to print (.html; open it and use Print → Save as PDF) or as Markdown text (.md).
  • Print several: in the ⋮ at the top press Select several, tick the exercises and choose Print sheet: they are put together on a single sheet, each with its statement, results and working.

What the browser saves is only in that browser. If you clear the browsing data or change device, use the backup in the ⋮ of My exercises.
