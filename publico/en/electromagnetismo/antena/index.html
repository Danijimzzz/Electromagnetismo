    Far field of a dipole

    A wire antenna carrying an alternating current of amplitude $I_0$ radiates spherical waves. Far from it ($r ≫ λ$ and $r ≫ 2L^2/λ$) the electric field has only a $θ̂$ component and decays as $1/r$:

    $|E| = {η_0 I_0}/{2π r} F(θ)$,   $|B| = |E|/c$,   $S = {|E|^2}/{2η_0}$

    The angle θ is measured from the antenna axis and $η_0 = 376.73 Ω$. The shape of the pattern $F(θ)$ depends on the length $L$:

    Short dipole ($L ≤ λ/10$): $F(θ) = π {L}/{λ} sin θ$

    Arbitrary length: $F(θ) = {cos(π {L}/{λ} cos θ) − cos(π {L}/{λ})}/{sin θ}$

    For the half-wave dipole ($L = λ/2$) this gives $F = cos(π/2 cos θ)/sin θ$. The pattern does not radiate along the axis and is maximum in the plane perpendicular to the antenna; if $L > λ$ side lobes appear.

    Power, radiation resistance and directivity

    The radiated power is the flux of the Poynting vector through a sphere, and the radiation resistance $R_"rad"$ is the resistance that would dissipate that same power with the current $I_0$:

    $P_"rad" = {η_0 I_0^2}/{4π} ∫_0^π F^2 sin θ dθ = 1/2 R_"rad" I_0^2$

    For the short dipole $R_"rad" = 80 π^2 (L/λ)^2$ and for the half-wave dipole $R_"rad" ≈ 73.1 Ω$. The directivity compares the direction of maximum radiation with an isotropic antenna radiating the same power:

    $D = {4π U_"max"}/{P_"rad"} = {2 F_"max"^2}/{∫_0^π F^2 sin θ dθ}$,   $D_"dBi" = 10 log_10 D$

    $D = 1.5$ (1.76 dBi) for the short dipole and $D ≈ 1.64$ (2.15 dBi) for the half-wave one. The −3 dB beamwidth is the angle between the two directions where the power drops to half of the maximum: 90° and 78° respectively. The current $I_0$ is the amplitude at the centre of the half-wave dipole and that of the current-wave maximum in the others.

    Frequently asked questions

    What radiation resistance and directivity does a half-wave dipole have?

    Its radiation resistance is about 73 Ω and its directivity 1.64 (2.15 dBi). The radiation pattern is proportional to [cos(π/2·cos θ)/sin θ]², with its maximum perpendicular to the wire and a null along its axis.

    How is the radiated power calculated?

    P_rad = ½·I₀²·R_rad. A half-wave dipole with I₀ = 1 A radiates ½ · 1² · 73 ≈ 36.5 W. For a short (Hertzian) dipole, R_rad = 80π²(l/λ)².
