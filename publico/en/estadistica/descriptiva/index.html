    Measures of central tendency

    The mean is the centre of gravity of the data, $x̄ = {∑ x_i n_i}/N$. The median leaves half the data on each side and is not affected by extreme values, and the mode is the value that appears most often. With grouped data the class mark of each interval is used and the median is interpolated:

    $Me = L_i + {N/2 − N_{i−1}}/{n_i} · a_i$

    Measures of position

    The quartiles $Q_1$, $Q_2 = Me$ and $Q_3$ leave 25 %, 50 % and 75 % of the data below, and the percentile $P_k$, k %. With ungrouped data the position $k·N/4$ is looked up in the cumulative frequencies; if it coincides exactly with one of them, the mean of that value and the next is taken. There are other conventions (calculators and spreadsheets interpolate), and they may give slightly different values.

    Measures of dispersion

    The variance is the mean of the squared deviations from the mean, and the standard deviation its root, in the same units as the data. With a sample, to estimate that of the population we divide by $N − 1$ (sample variance):

    $σ^2 = {∑ (x_i − x̄)^2 n_i}/N$, $s^2 = {∑ (x_i − x̄)^2 n_i}/{N − 1}$

    The coefficient of variation, $CV = {σ}/{|x̄|}$, compares the dispersion of variables with different units or means.

    Box plot and outliers

    The box runs from the first to the third quartile, with the median inside. A data point is an outlier if it is below $Q_1 − 1.5 · IQR$ or above $Q_3 + 1.5 · IQR$, with $IQR = Q_3 − Q_1$; the whiskers reach the most extreme data point that is not one.

    Other means: geometric, harmonic and weighted

    The arithmetic mean is not always the right one. When the data are multiplied together (growth rates, indices, interest over several years) the geometric mean is used; when they are ratios with the same numerator (speeds over legs of equal length, yields) the harmonic. They only make sense with positive data and always come in this order: harmonic ≤ geometric ≤ arithmetic. In the weighted mean each data point counts according to its weight, like marks weighted by the credits of each subject.

    $G = (x_1 · x_2 ⋯ x_N)^{1/N}$,   $H = N/{∑ 1/x_i}$,   $x̄_p = {∑ p_i x_i}/{∑ p_i}$

    Frequently asked questions

    How are the mean, the median and the standard deviation calculated?

    With the data 2, 3, 3, 5, 7: mean 4, median 3 and mode 3. The sample variance is s² = Σ(x − x̄)²/(n − 1) = 16/4 = 4, so s = 2.

    How are outliers detected?

    With the quartiles: IQR = Q₃ − Q₁, and outliers are the values outside [Q₁ − 1.5·IQR, Q₃ + 1.5·IQR], which are also the ends of the whiskers of the box plot.
