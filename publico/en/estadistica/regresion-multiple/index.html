    The model

    $Y = β_0 + β_1 X_1 + β_2 X_2 + ⋯ + β_p X_p + ε$. Each $β_j$ is the mean change in $Y$ when $X_j$ rises by one unit holding the other variables constant, and so it does not coincide with the slope of the simple regression of $Y$ on $X_j$ if the $X$ are correlated with each other.

    Least squares

    The coefficients that minimize the sum of squared residuals satisfy the normal equations:

    $(X^T X) β̂ = X^T y$,   $β̂ = (X^T X)^{-1} X^T y$

    with $X$ the matrix with a column of ones and one column per variable. The residual variance is $s^2 = SS_{res}/(n − p − 1)$ and the error of each coefficient, $s √([(X^T X)^{-1}]_{jj})$.

    Advertising

    Fit and tests

    $R^2 = 1 − SS_{res}/SS_T$ never drops when variables are added; the adjusted $R^2$, $1 − (1 − R^2)(n − 1)/(n − p − 1)$, penalizes those that contribute nothing. Each $t_j = β̂_j/SE(β̂_j)$ tests $β_j = 0$ and the global $F$ tests that all the $β_j$ (except the constant) are zero at once.

    Careful

    If two explanatory variables are closely related (multicollinearity) the errors of their coefficients blow up and the signs can be misleading. More observations than coefficients are needed: $n > p + 1$.

    Frequently asked questions

    How are the coefficients of a multiple linear regression calculated?

    By least squares, solving the normal equations (XᵀX)β = Xᵀy, that is, β̂ = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy. Each coefficient is the mean change in Y when that variable rises by one unit while the others are held constant.

    What is the difference between R² and adjusted R²?

    R² = 1 − SS_res/SS_T never drops when variables are added; adjusted R² = 1 − (1 − R²)(n − 1)/(n − p − 1) penalizes those that contribute nothing. The global F tests that all the coefficients (except the constant) are 0 at once.
