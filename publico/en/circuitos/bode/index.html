    What a Bode plot is

    A filter lets some frequencies through and attenuates others. Its response is the transfer function $H(jω) = V_"out"/V_"in"$, a complex number for each frequency. The Bode plot draws its magnitude in decibels, $20 log |H|$, and its phase, both against frequency on a logarithmic scale. This way, the responses of cascaded filters add up and the asymptotic lines can be drawn by hand.

    First-order filters

    An RC or an RL has a single cutoff frequency, where the gain drops 3 dB ($|H| = 1/√2$) and the phase is ∓45°:

    $f_c = 1/(2πRC) = R/(2πL)$

    Above (low-pass) or below (high-pass) $f_c$ the gain changes by 20 dB per decade. An RC low-pass takes its output across the capacitor; an RC high-pass, across the resistor.

    Second-order RLC filters

    The series RLC has a natural frequency and a quality factor that sets what the response looks like near it:

    $f_0 = 1/(2π√(LC))$, $Q = (1/R)√(L/C)$, $"BW" = f_0/Q$

    With $Q > 1/√2$ the low-pass and high-pass show a resonance peak; in the band-pass (measured across the resistor) $Q$ gives how narrow the band is, and in the band-stop, how narrow the notch is. The asymptotes change by 40 dB per decade in the low-pass and high-pass, and ±20 dB per decade in the band-pass.

    Group delay

    Phase measures how much each frequency is delayed; group delay, how much a signal made of nearby frequencies is delayed, such as the envelope of a pulse. It is the derivative of the phase:

    $τ_g = −dφ/dω$

    For an RC low-pass it is $τ_g = RC/(1 + (ωRC)²)$: $RC$ at DC and half of that at the cutoff frequency. A second-order filter with high $Q$ delays frequencies near $f_0$ a lot ($τ_g = 2Q/ω_0$ in the low-pass RLC), which distorts pulses.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do you calculate the cutoff frequency of an RC filter?

    f_c = 1/(2πRC), where the gain drops 3 dB. With R = 1 kΩ and C = 159 nF, f_c ≈ 1 kHz, and beyond that it falls 20 dB per decade.

    And the resonant frequency of an RLC?

    f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)); in series, the quality factor is Q = (1/R)√(L/C). With L = 10 mH and C = 100 nF, f₀ ≈ 5.03 kHz.
