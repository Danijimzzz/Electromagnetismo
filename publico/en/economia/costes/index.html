    Fixed and variable costs

    Fixed costs (FC) do not depend on how much is produced: rent, insurance, machinery. Variable costs (VC) grow with output: raw materials, energy, overtime. Total cost is TC = FC + VC.

    Average costs and marginal cost

    Average costs are the cost per unit: $ATC = {TC}/Q = AFC + AVC$, with $AFC = {FC}/Q$ and $AVC = {VC}/Q$. Marginal cost is what it costs to produce one more unit, $MC = {dTC}/{dQ}$.

    Advertising

    The relationship between marginal and average

    If marginal cost is below average cost, producing more lowers it; if it is above, it raises it. That is why marginal cost crosses average cost (and average variable cost) exactly at their minimums. Average fixed cost only falls: it is spread over more units.

    Shutdown point and efficient scale

    The shutdown point is the minimum of average variable cost: at a lower price, not even the variable costs are covered and the firm shuts down. The efficient scale is the minimum of average total cost, where $MC = ATC$: it is the long-run break-even threshold, and at prices below it there are losses.

    The function used by the calculator

    $TC(Q) = FC + a · Q + b · Q^2 + c · Q^3$. With $b < 0$ and $c > 0$ the textbook U-shaped curve results (first increasing returns, then decreasing); with $c = 0$ and $b > 0$, a parabola.

    Frequently asked questions

    How are average and marginal cost calculated?

    ATC = TC/Q and MC = dTC/dQ. With TC = 100 + 10Q + Q² and Q = 10: TC = 300, ATC = 30 and MC = 10 + 2Q = 30.

    What are the efficient scale and the shutdown point?

    The efficient scale is the minimum of average total cost, where MC = ATC (in the example, Q = 10). The shutdown point is the minimum of average variable cost, the price below which the firm stops producing.
