    Simple interest

    Interest is always calculated on the initial capital, so it is the same every year and the capital grows in a straight line. It is used in operations of less than a year, such as bills or discounts.

    $C_n = C_0 (1 + i · t)$, $I = C_0 · i · t$

    Compound interest

    The interest of each period is added to the capital and generates interest in the next period too: the capital grows exponentially. With m periods per year (12 if monthly) and a nominal rate i:

    $C_n = C_0 (1 + i/m)^{m · t}$

    Solving gives the initial capital (discounting), $C_0 = C_n (1 + i)^{−t}$, the rate, $i = (C_n/C_0)^{1/t} − 1$, or the time, $t = {ln(C_n/C_0)}/{ln(1 + i)}$.

    TIN and TAE

    The TIN (nominal interest rate) is the annual rate that is split among the periods: $i_m = TIN/m$. The TAE (effective annual rate, similar to the APR or EAR) is what it really yields in a year, counting interest on interest:

    $TAE = (1 + TIN/m)^m − 1$

    That is why banks are required to state the TAE: it allows products with different payment schedules to be compared. For loans, the TAE also includes mandatory fees and expenses.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

    Simple: Cₙ = C₀(1 + i·n); compound: Cₙ = C₀(1 + i)ⁿ. With €1,000 at 5 % for 3 years: €1,150 with simple interest and €1,157.63 with compound interest.

    How do you go from the TIN to the TAE?

    TAE = (1 + i_m)^m − 1. A TIN (nominal annual rate) of 6 % with monthly payment is 0.5 % per month and gives a TAE (effective annual rate, equivalent to the APR/EAR) of 1.005¹² − 1 ≈ 6.17 %.
