    The payoff matrix

    Two players choose at the same time, without knowing what the other does, one of their two strategies. Each cell of the matrix says how much each one earns with that combination: first player 1 (rows) and then player 2 (columns).

    Dominant strategy and Nash equilibrium

    A strategy is dominant if it pays more than the other whatever happens. A Nash equilibrium is a combination in which nobody wants to change on their own: each plays their best response to what the other does. If there are dominant strategies, the equilibrium uses them.

    Mixed equilibrium

    Sometimes it pays to decide at random. If player 2 plays their first strategy with probability $q$, player 1 is indifferent between theirs when their two expected payoffs coincide, and $q$ follows from that; likewise with $p$ for player 2:

    $q = {a_{22} − a_{12}}/{a_{11} − a_{12} − a_{21} + a_{22}}$

    $p = {b_{22} − b_{21}}/{b_{11} − b_{12} − b_{21} + b_{22}}$

    Efficiency and the prisoner's dilemma

    An outcome is Pareto optimal if no one can be made better off without making the other worse off. A Nash equilibrium need not be: in the prisoner's dilemma both confess because it is the best each can do, even though both would be better off if they stayed silent. The same happens with two firms that cut prices, or with countries that do not reduce their emissions.

    Limits of this tool

    • These are simultaneous two-player games: there is no sequential decision tree (subgame perfection) and no more than three strategies per player.
    • In the 3 × 3 matrix the pure-strategy Nash equilibrium is found and strictly dominated strategies are eliminated by a pure strategy; the mixed equilibrium is only calculated in 2 × 2.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a Nash equilibrium?

    A combination of strategies in which no player gains by changing only their own. In the prisoner's dilemma, the equilibrium is for both to confess (5 and 5 years) even though both staying silent (1 and 1) would be better for both.

    How is it found in a 2×2 matrix?

    Mark each player's best response to each strategy of the other; the cells with both marks are Nash equilibria.
