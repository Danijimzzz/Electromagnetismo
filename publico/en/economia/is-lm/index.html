IS-LM model

Calculate the equilibrium of an economy in the goods market (IS) and the money market (LM), and see how income and the interest rate change with fiscal and monetary policy.

    The IS curve: equilibrium in the goods market

    Output equals demand: $Y = C + I + G$. Consumption depends on disposable income, $C = c_0 + c_1 (Y − T)$, and investment falls when the interest rate rises, $I = I_0 − b · i$. Solving for the rate gives a downward-sloping line: the cheaper credit is, the more is invested and the more income needs to be produced.

    $Y = {c_0 − c_1 T + I_0 + G − b · i}/{1 − c_1}$

    The LM curve: equilibrium in the money market

    The real money supply $M/P$ equals money demand, $L = k · Y − h · i$: more money is demanded when income is higher (to pay for transactions) and less when the rate, which is the cost of holding it instead of bonds, is high. The LM curve slopes upward.

    $M/P = k · Y − h · i$

    The equilibrium and the multipliers

    Where the two curves cross, both markets are in equilibrium at once:

    $Y^* = {A + b · M/P / h}/{(1 − c_1) + b · k / h}$

    An increase in autonomous spending $A$ shifts the IS curve to the right, but income rises less than with the Keynesian multiplier $1/(1 − c_1)$: more income raises the rate and the rate holds back investment (crowding out). An increase in the real money supply shifts the LM curve down, lowers the rate and stimulates investment and income. If investment does not react to the rate (small b) fiscal policy is very effective; if money demand is very sensitive to the rate (huge h, liquidity trap) the LM curve is almost flat: fiscal policy works with its full multiplier and monetary policy does almost nothing.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is the IS-LM equilibrium found?

    The two curves are solved together. With IS: Y = 2000 − 100r and LM: Y = 1000 + 100r, setting them equal gives r = 5 and Y = 1500.

    What effect do fiscal and monetary policy have?

    A fiscal expansion shifts the IS curve to the right and raises income and the interest rate. A monetary expansion shifts the LM curve to the right and raises income and lowers the interest rate.
