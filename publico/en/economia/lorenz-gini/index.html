    Lorenz curve

    The population is sorted from lowest to highest income and, for each cumulative fraction of the population $p$, the fraction of income it holds, $q$, is plotted. If everyone earned the same, 20 % of people would have 20 % of income and the curve would be the diagonal; the further it departs from it downward, the more unequal the distribution.

    Gini index

    It is twice the area between the diagonal and the curve: 0 if everyone has the same and almost 1 if a single person has everything. With the points of the curve it is computed by trapezoids, $G = 1 − Σ (p_i − p_{i−1})(q_i + q_{i−1})$. Many textbooks use $I_G = {Σ (p_i − q_i)}/{Σ p_i}$, which gives almost the same when there are many groups. Spain is around 0.32; the Nordic countries, close to 0.25; South Africa, above 0.6.

    S80/S20 ratio

    It divides the income of the richest 20 % by that of the poorest 20 %. It is the other inequality measure published by the INE (Spain's national statistics institute) and Eurostat, and it is understood without formulas: a 5 means the richest fifth has five times the income of the poorest.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is the Gini index calculated?

    G = 1 − Σ(xᵢ − xᵢ₋₁)(yᵢ + yᵢ₋₁), with x and y the cumulative proportions of population and income. For 4 people with incomes 10, 20, 30 and 40: y = 0.1; 0.3; 0.6; 1, and G = 1 − 0.25 · 3.0 = 0.25.

    What is the Lorenz curve?

    It shows what cumulative percentage of income is held by each cumulative percentage of the population. With perfect equality it is the diagonal (G = 0); the more it curves, the greater the inequality (G close to 1).
