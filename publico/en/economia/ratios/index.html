    The balance sheet and working capital

    Assets are ordered from least to most liquid: non-current (what stays more than a year), inventories, receivables (what is to be collected) and cash (cash and banks). They are financed with equity (the owners') and with liabilities, non-current (debts due in more than a year) and current (due in less than a year).

    Working capital, $WC = CA − CL$, is the part of current assets financed in the long term. If it is negative, the company cannot pay its short-term debts with what it has in the short term.

    Liquidity and solvency

    Liquidity ratios compare current assets with short-term debts (current ratio, between 1.5 and 2; quick ratio, excluding inventories, between 0.75 and 1; cash ratio, with cash only, between 0.1 and 0.3). The solvency ratio compares all assets with all debts (between 1.5 and 2.5). The recommended values are indicative: they depend a lot on the sector.

    Leverage

    The debt ratio, the part of assets financed with debt, should be between 0.4 and 0.6. Autonomy compares equity with debts, and debt quality says what part falls due in the short term: the lower, the better.

    Returns and leverage

    Return on assets, $ROA = EBIT/TA$, measures what the assets yield; it is margin times turnover. Return on equity, $ROE = {"net income"}/{E}$, what the owners' money yields. If debt costs less than the assets yield ($i < ROA$), borrowing raises the ROE: this is financial leverage, $ROE = [ROA + (ROA − i) L/E](1 − t)$.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is liquidity calculated?

    Current ratio = current assets/current liabilities. With 300 of current assets and 200 of current liabilities, the ratio is 1.5, above 1: the company can pay its short-term debts.

    And the returns ROE and ROA?

    ROE = net profit/equity; ROA = profit/total assets. With a profit of 50, equity of 400 and assets of 1000: ROE = 12.5 % and ROA = 5 %.
