    French system

    It is the one used for almost all mortgages and personal loans: the payment $a$ is always the same. It is calculated so that the present value of the $n$ payments, at the rate $i$ per period, equals the amount borrowed:

    $a = {C · i}/{1 − (1 + i)^{−n}}$

    In each payment, the interest is what is owed at the rate, $I_k = C_{k−1} · i$, and the rest repays principal. At first the debt is large and almost everything is interest; at the end, almost everything is principal.

    Constant-principal payment

    Each period the same part of the principal is repaid, $A = C/n$, plus the interest on what remains. The payment starts high and falls each period, and less interest is paid in total than under the French system.

    American system

    Only interest is paid during the life of the loan and the entire principal is repaid at the end, in one go. It is the system of many bonds and debentures.

    TIN and TAE on a loan

    The rate for each period is $i = TIN/m$ (with m = 12 if paid monthly). The TAE is the annual rate that equates what is received with what is paid: if there is an opening fee, less money is received for the same payments and the TAE rises.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is the payment of a loan calculated under the French system?

    a = C·i/(1 − (1 + i)⁻ⁿ). With €10,000 at 6 % per year (0.5 % per month) over 12 months, a = 10,000 · 0.005/(1 − 1.005⁻¹²) ≈ €860.66.

    How is each payment split between interest and principal?

    Interest is the rate times the outstanding principal. In the first payment of the example: €50 of interest and €810.66 of principal. In the constant-principal system the same C/n is always repaid (€833.33 in the example) and the payment goes down.
