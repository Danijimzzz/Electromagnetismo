    Markowitz's idea

    A portfolio with weights $w$ yields $μ_p = w^T·μ$ and has risk $σ_p = √(w^T·Σ·w)$. Since assets do not move entirely together (correlation below 1), splitting the money lowers the risk without lowering the return: that is diversification. Of all possible portfolios, the efficient ones are those that give the highest return for each level of risk; together they form the efficient frontier. The minimum-variance one is its leftmost point.

    Risk-free asset and tangency portfolio

    If there is also a risk-free asset with return $r_f$, the best risky portfolio is the one that maximizes the Sharpe ratio $S = (μ_p − r_f)/σ_p$, the tangency portfolio. Mixing it with the risk-free asset traces the capital market line $μ_p = r_f + S·σ_p$, and all investors choose the same risky portfolio; only how much they lend or borrow changes.

    CAPM

    $μ_i = r_f + β_i·(μ_M − r_f)$, $β_i = σ_{iM}/σ_M^2$

    If the market is the tangency portfolio, the return the market requires of an asset depends only on its beta, the part of its risk that cannot be eliminated by diversifying. The return-beta line is the security market line (SML). The alpha $α_i = μ_i − (r_f + β_i(μ_M − r_f))$ measures how much more or less it yields relative to that line.

    Short selling

    With short selling the weights can be negative and everything has a closed-form solution. Without it ($w_i ≥ 0$) you have to test which assets enter the portfolio; the tool does so with every possible group, so the result is exact. It is a theoretical model: it uses estimates of return and risk that change, and it is not investment advice.

    What this model assumes

    • Expected returns, risks and correlations are data you enter, not estimates made by the tool (in practice they are estimated from historical series and fail out of sample).
    • A single period, with the same return distribution for all.
    • You can lend and borrow at the same risk-free rate rf (the tangency portfolio and the CML depend on this).
    • No transaction costs, no taxes and infinitely divisible assets.
    • Constant correlations, and the result is exact only within the model: without short selling all groups of assets are tested (up to 6); with short selling they come from the closed form.
    • By default short selling is not allowed (weights ≥ 0, no leverage); with the «Allowed» option the weights can be negative. The active mode always appears in the results.
    • The target return is optional; without short selling it must lie between that of the lowest-yielding asset and that of the highest. With two assets, the correlation can be +1 or −1 (special classroom cases).

    Limits of this tool

    • It works with 2 to 6 assets and with the returns, volatilities and correlations you enter: it does not estimate them from historical prices.
    • It is the single-period mean-variance model: it does not take into account fees, taxes or per-asset weight limits (only allowing or not short selling).

    Frequently asked questions

    How is the minimum-variance portfolio of two assets calculated?

    w₁ = (σ₂² − ρσ₁σ₂)/(σ₁² + σ₂² − 2ρσ₁σ₂). With σ₁ = 20 %, σ₂ = 10 % and ρ = 0.3: w₁ = 0.004/0.038 ≈ 10.5 % and w₂ ≈ 89.5 %; the risk falls to σ ≈ 9.79 %, less than that of the safer asset.

    What is the tangency portfolio and how is the CAPM obtained?

    The tangency portfolio maximizes the Sharpe ratio (μ − rf)/σ. If the market is that portfolio, the return required of an asset is rf + β·(μM − rf), with β = σiM/σM². An asset with an expected return above that figure has a positive alpha.
