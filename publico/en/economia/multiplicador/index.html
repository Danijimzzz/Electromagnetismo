    The income-expenditure model

    In the short run, with fixed prices and unemployed resources, output is set by demand. What is produced is what is expected to be sold, and equilibrium income is the one that equates output and aggregate demand: $Y = AD = C + I + G + X − M$.

    The Keynesian cross

    Aggregate demand grows with income because consumption depends on it: $AD = A + b · Y$. The 45° line marks the points where output and demand coincide, and equilibrium income $Y^* = {A}/{1 − b}$ is where they cross. If output is above it, firms build up inventories and cut back; if below, they run them down and produce more.

    The spending multiplier

    An extra euro of autonomous spending becomes someone's income, who spends a fraction $b$ of it, which is another person's income, who spends a fraction $b$… The sum is $1 + b + b^2 + … = {1}/{1 − b}$. The more is consumed (high $c$), the less is paid in taxes (low $t$) and the less is imported (low $m$), the larger the multiplier. With $b = c · (1 − t) − m$.

    Taxes and the balanced budget

    An extra euro of lump-sum taxes cuts consumption by $c$ euros, not by one, so its multiplier is smaller in absolute value: $k_T = −{c}/{1 − b}$. That is why raising spending and taxes by the same amount increases income: this is the balanced-budget theorem, with multiplier $k_{eq} = {1 − c}/{1 − b}$ (equal to 1 in the simplest model).

    Output gap

    Equilibrium income need not coincide with full-employment income. If it is lower there is a recessionary gap, and the government can close it with more spending or lower taxes: the spending needed is the gap divided by the multiplier. The model assumes fixed prices and spare capacity; near full employment, part of the effect would go into inflation.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is the spending multiplier calculated?

    k = 1/(1 − c), where c is the marginal propensity to consume (with proportional taxes, 1/(1 − c(1 − t))). With c = 0.8, k = 5: an increase in spending of 10 raises income by 50.

    How is equilibrium income found?

    From Y = C + I + G. With C = 100 + 0.8Y, I = 50 and G = 50: Y = 200 + 0.8Y, so Y = 1000.
