      Molar mass

      The molar mass M of a substance is the mass of one mole of its particles, in g/mol. It is obtained by adding the atomic masses of all the atoms in the formula: for water, M(H2O) = 2 · 1.008 + 15.999 = 18.015 g/mol. The percent composition is the percentage of the mass contributed by each element.

      Balancing reactions

      Balancing a reaction means finding the coefficients that conserve the number of atoms of each element (and the charge, if there are ions). Each element gives a linear equation; the coefficients are the smallest integer solution.

      Limiting reagent and yield

      • Convert each amount to moles: n = m / M, or n = V / 22.4 for a gas at standard conditions.
      • Divide the moles of each reactant by its coefficient. The smallest quotient marks the limiting reagent: it is used up entirely and decides how much product forms.
      • The moles of product come from the ratio of coefficients with the limiting reagent. If the yield is not 100 %, multiply by it.
      • Of the other reactants, what does not react is left over: the excess reagent.

      If a reagent is not pure, only its purity counts: of 10 g at 90 %, 9 g react. If it is in solution, n = M · V. A gas not at standard conditions occupies V = nRT/P, with R = 0.082 atm·L/(mol·K). If you know how much product was actually obtained, the yield is the actual mass divided by the theoretical one, times 100.

      Empirical and molecular formula

      • Convert the composition to grams (with 100 g of compound, each % is grams) and the grams to moles of each element.
      • Divide all by the smallest. If any is not a whole number (1.5; 1.33; 1.25…), multiply all by 2, 3 or 4.
      • Those numbers are the subscripts of the empirical formula. The molecular one is a multiple of it: n = M / Mempirical.
      • In a combustion analysis all the carbon ends up as CO2 and all the hydrogen as H2O; the oxygen is what is missing up to the mass of the sample.

      Frequently asked questions

      How do you calculate the molar mass and the moles of a substance?

      The molar mass is the sum of the atomic masses in the formula, and n = m/M. For H₂O, M = 2 · 1.008 + 16.00 = 18.02 g/mol, and 36 g is 36 / 18.02 ≈ 2 mol.

      How do you find the limiting reagent?

      Divide the moles of each reactant by its stoichiometric coefficient and the smallest quotient points to the limiting reagent. In 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O with 3 mol of H₂ and 2 mol of O₂: 3/2 = 1.5 and 2/1 = 2, so the limiting reagent is H₂ and 3 mol of water form. The yield is what is obtained divided by the theoretical amount, times 100.
