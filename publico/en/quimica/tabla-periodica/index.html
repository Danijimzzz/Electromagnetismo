How to read the periodic table

Elements are ordered by atomic number Z, the number of protons. Each row is a period: all its elements have their valence electrons in the same shell. Each column is a group: its elements have the same valence-shell configuration and so are chemically similar. The lanthanides and actinides are shown in two separate rows so the table is not too wide.

Periodic trends

  • Atomic radius: increases going down a group, because shells are added, and decreases to the right across a period, because the effective nuclear charge grows.
  • Ionization energy: the energy needed to remove the outermost electron. It follows the opposite trend to the radius: it grows going up and to the right, with the maximum at the noble gases.
  • Electronegativity: the tendency to attract the electrons of a bond. It grows going up and to the right; fluorine is the most electronegative (3.98 on the Pauling scale).
  • Electron affinity: the energy released when an electron is gained. It is large for the halogens (chlorine, 349 kJ/mol, more than fluorine), while noble gases and alkaline earth metals do not form stable anions.
  • Metallic character: grows going down and to the left. The metalloids form the border between metals and nonmetals.

Group 3 and the f block

For years there was debate about whether lanthanum and actinium or lutetium and lawrencium go below yttrium. IUPAC recommends (2021 report) Sc, Y, Lu and Lr in group 3, with La–Yb and Ac–No in the f block, which then has 14 columns. Here the 15 lanthanides and 15 actinides are drawn in their separate rows, as in many textbooks; the card for La, Ac, Lu and Lr notes this.

Electron configuration

The card shows the ground-state configuration according to Madelung's rule (filling order by n + l), with the well-known exceptions such as chromium ([Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹) or copper ([Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹), and the distribution of electrons by shell.

Atomic masses: abridged IUPAC values; in square brackets, the mass number of the most stable isotope. Radii: calculated by Clementi and Raimondi (1967). The heaviest elements have no measured properties.

Frequently asked questions

How do you read the periodic table?

Elements in the same column (group) have the same number of valence electrons, and each row (period) corresponds to a shell. Atomic radius grows going down and to the left; ionization energy and electronegativity grow going up and to the right.

What data does each element's card give?

Atomic number, atomic mass, electron configuration, electronegativity, oxidation states, melting and boiling points and density. With them you can compare trends of elements in the same group or period.
