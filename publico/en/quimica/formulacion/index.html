    Three ways to name a compound

    The same compound has several valid names, depending on the nomenclature used. In the systematic one, numerical prefixes say how many atoms there are of each element: Fe₂O₃ is diiron trioxide. In the Stock one, the oxidation state of the element that can have several is written in parentheses, in Roman numerals: Fe₂O₃ is iron(III) oxide. In the traditional one, suffixes and prefixes are used: iron with +2 is “ferrous” and with +3 “ferric”, so Fe₂O₃ is ferric oxide.

    For oxoacids and their salts the traditional one is the most used (sulfuric acid, sodium sulfate), but IUPAC recommends the systematic one: disodium tetraoxidosulfate.

    Numerical prefixes

    1 mono-, 2 di-, 3 tri-, 4 tetra-, 5 penta-, 6 hexa-, 7 hepta-. If what repeats is a group of several atoms (such as sulfate, SO₄) bis- (2), tris- (3) and tetrakis- (4) are used and the group goes in parentheses: dialuminium tris(tetraoxidosulfate). The prefix mono- of the first element is always omitted.

    From name to formula: criss-crossing the charges

    The cation (the metal or the less electronegative element) is written first and then the anion. The charge of one becomes the subscript of the other and it is simplified if possible. With Fe³⁺ and SO₄²⁻ we get Fe₂(SO₄)₃, and the compound is neutral because

    $2·(+3) + 3·(−2) = 0$

    The SO₄ group takes parentheses because it is repeated three times; if the subscript were 1 there are no parentheses or number.

    Endings of traditional nomenclature

    When an element forms two oxoacids, the one with the lower oxidation state ends in -ous (its salts in -ite) and the one with the higher in -ic (salts in -ate): H₂SO₃ is sulfurous acid and its salts the sulfites; H₂SO₄ is sulfuric acid and its salts the sulfates. The halogens have four: hypo-…-ous (+1), -ous (+3), -ic (+5) and per-…-ic (+7), as in HClO, HClO₂, HClO₃ and HClO₄.

    Acid salts

    If an acid with several hydrogens gives up only some, the anion keeps the rest and is named with “hydrogen” in front (and di-, tri- if there are several): NaHCO₃ is sodium hydrogencarbonate and Ca(H₂PO₄)₂ calcium bis(dihydrogenphosphate).

    Limits of this tool

    • Coordination compounds only admit one central metal, with the metals and ligands in the tool's table: it does not name complexes with several metals, bridging ligands or isomers (cis/trans, fac/mer).
    • The name-to-formula step for a complex looks in a list of about 50 complexes from the syllabus; it does not interpret any name.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do you name an inorganic compound?

    There are three nomenclatures: systematic (prefixes), Stock (Roman numeral) and traditional. Fe₂O₃ is diiron trioxide, iron(III) oxide or ferric oxide; CO₂ is carbon dioxide.

    And hydroxides, acids and salts?

    Ca(OH)₂ is calcium hydroxide, H₂SO₄ is sulfuric acid (dihydrogen tetraoxidosulfate) and NaCl is sodium chloride. The tool goes from formula to name and from name to formula.
