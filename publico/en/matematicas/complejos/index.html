    The four forms of a complex number

    A complex number z = a + bi has real part a and imaginary part b, with $i^2 = −1$. It is drawn as the point (a, b) of the complex plane (its affix) or as the arrow from the origin to it. That arrow has a length, the modulus r, and makes an angle with the real axis, the argument θ:

    $r = |z| = √(a^2 + b^2)$,   $tan θ = b/a$

    • Rectangular: $z = a + bi$
    • Polar: $z = r_θ$ (also written r∠θ)
    • Trigonometric: $z = r(cos θ + i sin θ)$, with $a = r cos θ$ and $b = r sin θ$
    • Exponential: $z = r e^{iθ}$, by Euler's formula $e^{iθ} = cos θ + i sin θ$, with θ in radians

    Watch the quadrant. The calculator gives arctan(b/a) between −90° and 90°, so if a < 0 (second or third quadrant) you have to add or subtract 180°. For example, −1 + i and 1 − i both give arctan(−1) = −45°, but the argument of −1 + i is 135°.

    Operations

    To add and subtract, the rectangular form is used: the real and imaginary parts are added (it is the parallelogram rule). To multiply and divide, the polar form is more convenient:

    $r_α · s_β = (r s)_{α + β}$,   $r_α / s_β = (r/s)_{α − β}$

    In rectangular form, the product is done like that of two binomials using $i^2 = −1$, and the quotient by multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator, $z̄ = a − bi$, because $z z̄ = a^2 + b^2$ is real.

    Powers and roots

    De Moivre's formula raises the modulus to the power and multiplies the argument:

    $(r_θ)^n = (r^n)_{nθ}$

    A nonzero complex number has exactly n nth roots, all with the same modulus and spaced every 360°/n, at the vertices of a regular polygon:

    $(r_θ)^{1/n} = (r^{1/n})_{(θ + 360° k)/n}$,   $k = 0, 1, …, n − 1$

    Quadratic equations

    With complex numbers, every equation $a z^2 + b z + c = 0$ has two solutions, $z = {−b ± √Δ}/{2a}$. If the coefficients are real and $Δ = b^2 − 4ac < 0$, the solutions are complex conjugates, because $√Δ = i √(−Δ)$.

    Complex logarithm, exponential and powers

    The exponential of z = a + bi is ez = ea(cos b + i sin b): it has modulus ea and argument b, and repeats every 2πi. That is why the logarithm has infinitely many values, ln z = ln|z| + i(θ + 2kπ); the principal one is the one with −π < θ ≤ π. With it, powers with complex exponent are defined, zw = ew ln z: for example, ii = e−π/2 is a real number.

    Polynomial equations and regions

    A polynomial equation of degree n has exactly n complex solutions, counting repeated ones (fundamental theorem of algebra). The rational ones are found with Ruffini's rule among the divisors of the constant term, and if the coefficients are real, the complex ones come in conjugate pairs. In the plane, |z − a| = r is the circle with center a and radius r, |z − a| < r its interior, Re z > c a half-plane and |z − a| = |z − b| the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining a and b.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do you convert a complex number from rectangular to polar form?

    With r = √(a² + b²) and θ = atan2(b, a). For 1 + i, r = √2 and θ = 45°, that is, √2·e^(iπ/4).

    How are the roots of a complex number calculated?

    ⁿ√z = r^(1/n)·e^(i(θ + 2πk)/n), with k = 0, …, n − 1 (De Moivre's formula). The cube roots of 8 are 2, −1 + √3·i and −1 − √3·i.
